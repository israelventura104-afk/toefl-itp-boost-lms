TQB-LIST-0136 — Recording Script (Part C)
Topic: Bird Migration and Navigation
Voices: Narrator + Woman (Biology Professor)
Target length: ~60–90 seconds

Narrator: Listen to part of a talk in a biology class.

Woman (Professor): Every year, millions of birds travel between breeding grounds and wintering sites.

Some species fly thousands of kilometers, often at night and over open water.

How do they navigate? Researchers think birds use several cues at once.

The position of the sun and stars can serve as a compass for daytime and nighttime flights.

Many birds also sense Earth’s magnetic field, which gives them a sense of direction even when skies are cloudy.

Landmarks matter too: coastlines, mountain ranges, and rivers help experienced birds stay on route.

Young birds sometimes learn routes by following their parents on the first journey.

Climate change and city lights can disrupt these systems, causing birds to arrive late or become disoriented.

So migration is not one trick—it is a toolkit of celestial, magnetic, and landscape cues.

Next week we’ll look at tracking studies that map exact migration highways.

Narrator: Now get ready to answer the questions.
