TQB-LIST-0138 — Recording Script (Part C)
Topic: Jazz and the Blues Roots
Voices: Narrator + Woman (Music History Professor)
Target length: ~60–90 seconds

Narrator: Listen to part of a talk in a music history class.

Woman (Professor): Early jazz did not appear from nowhere; it grew out of several African American musical traditions.

One of the strongest roots is the blues, with its expressive vocal style and flexible pitch.

Blues often uses a twelve-bar structure and “blue notes”—tones bent slightly for emotional color.

In New Orleans around 1900, brass bands, ragtime rhythms, and blues feeling mixed in dance halls and parades.

Improvisation became central: players invented phrases on the spot while staying within a shared form.

Instruments like the cornet, clarinet, and trombone took turns carrying the melody.

Recording technology later helped jazz travel far beyond its hometown scenes.

Still, many historians say you can hear blues sensibility inside jazz solos even today.

So when we study jazz history, we listen for those blues DNA strands—not just flashy technique.

Next time we’ll compare New Orleans style with the bigger swing bands of the 1930s.
