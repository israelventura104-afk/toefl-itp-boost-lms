TQB-LIST-0135 — Recording Script (Part C)
Topic: Ancient Roman Aqueducts
Voices: Narrator + Man (Archaeology Professor)
Target length: ~60–90 seconds

Narrator: Listen to part of a talk in an archaeology class.

Man (Professor): Roman cities needed huge amounts of fresh water for drinking, baths, and fountains.

Engineers solved that with aqueducts—channels that carried water from distant springs into town.

Most of an aqueduct ran underground or in covered trenches to keep the water clean and cool.

The famous arched bridges appear where the route had to cross a valley while keeping a gentle slope.

Gravity did the work: a slight downhill grade moved water for kilometers without pumps.

Inside the city, water reached public baths, street fountains, and sometimes private homes of the wealthy.

Maintenance crews cleared sediment and repaired leaks, because a break could cut supply for days.

Some aqueducts still stand, which tells us how carefully the Romans planned long-distance water systems.

So when you see those arches in photos, remember they were only one visible piece of a much longer network.

Next time we’ll compare Roman water technology with earlier systems in Greece and the Near East.
