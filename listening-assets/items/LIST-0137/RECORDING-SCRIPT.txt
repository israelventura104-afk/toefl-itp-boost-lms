TQB-LIST-0137 — Recording Script (Part C)
Topic: The Water Cycle and Climate
Voices: Narrator + Man (Earth Science Professor)
Target length: ~60–90 seconds

Narrator: Listen to part of a talk in an earth science class.

Man (Professor): Water on Earth moves in a continuous cycle among oceans, air, and land.

Heat from the sun evaporates water from oceans, lakes, and soil into water vapor.

As moist air rises and cools, vapor condenses into clouds, and precipitation returns water to the surface.

Some water runs off into rivers; some soaks into the ground and recharges aquifers.

Plants also release vapor through transpiration, which adds moisture to the air.

Climate warming can intensify parts of this cycle: warmer air holds more moisture, so some regions see heavier rain.

At the same time, other areas may face longer dry spells if storm tracks shift.

Glaciers and snowpack act as seasonal storage; when they shrink, summer river flow can drop.

So understanding the water cycle helps us predict both floods and droughts under a changing climate.

Next lecture we’ll map how local watersheds connect to this global system.
