TQB-LIST-0134 — Recording Script (Part C)
Topic: The Science of Habit Formation
Voices: Narrator + Woman (Psychology Professor)
Target length: ~60–90 seconds

Narrator: Listen to part of a talk in a psychology class.

Woman (Professor): A habit is a behavior that becomes automatic after repeated practice in a stable context.

Researchers often describe a simple loop: cue, routine, and reward.

The cue is a trigger—time of day, a place, or a feeling—that starts the behavior.

The routine is the action itself, and the reward is whatever makes your brain want to repeat it.

Once a habit is strong, you may start the routine with little conscious planning.

That efficiency saves mental energy, but it also makes unhealthy habits hard to break.

One evidence-based strategy is to keep the same cue and reward while swapping in a better routine.

For example, if stress cues afternoon snacking, you might replace chips with a short walk that still reduces tension.

Changing the environment—removing the cue—also helps more than relying on willpower alone.

Next class we’ll look at experiments on how long new habits typically take to stabilize.

Narrator: Now get ready to answer the questions.
