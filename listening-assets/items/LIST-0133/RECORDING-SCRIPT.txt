TQB-LIST-0133 — Recording Script (Part C)
Topic: Coral Reefs and Ocean Warming
Voices: Narrator + Man (Marine Biology Professor)
Target length: ~60–90 seconds

Narrator: Listen to part of a talk in a marine biology class.

Man (Professor): Coral reefs look like rocky gardens, but living coral animals build those structures over time.

Many corals depend on tiny algae that live inside their tissues and share energy from photosynthesis.

When ocean water stays unusually warm, that partnership breaks down—a process called bleaching.

The coral turns pale because it loses the colorful algae, and without them it can starve.

Some reefs recover if temperatures cool quickly, but repeated heat waves raise the risk of mass die-offs.

Bleaching also weakens habitat for fish that communities rely on for food and tourism.

Scientists track surface temperatures with satellites and send divers to count bleached colonies on site.

Protecting reefs means cutting greenhouse gases and reducing local stress from pollution and overfishing.

In short, coral health is an early warning system for how oceans respond to a warming climate.

Next week we’ll examine case studies from the Great Barrier Reef and the Caribbean.
