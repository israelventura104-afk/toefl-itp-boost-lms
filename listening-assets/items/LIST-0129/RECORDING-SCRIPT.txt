TQB-LIST-0129 — Recording Script (Part C)
Topic: Sleep Cycles and Memory
Voices: Narrator + Man (Psychology Professor)
Target length: ~60–90 seconds

Narrator: Listen to part of a talk in a psychology class.

Man (Professor): Many students think sleep is just “time off” for the brain. Research suggests the opposite.

During a normal night, we cycle through light sleep, deep sleep, and REM sleep several times.

Deep sleep seems especially important for stabilizing factual memories—the kind you need for dates, definitions, and formulas.

REM sleep, when most dreaming occurs, appears more linked to integrating ideas and practicing skills.

If you cut sleep short, you often lose more REM toward morning, which can hurt problem-solving the next day.

That is one reason an all-nighter can leave you able to recognize a term but unable to apply it on an essay.

Naps help a little, but they rarely replace a full set of night cycles.

Caffeine can make you feel alert without restoring the memory processes that happen during sleep.

So for exam weeks, consistent sleep may matter as much as another hour of review.

Next lecture we'll look at experiments that tested sleep and vocabulary learning in adults.

Narrator: Now get ready to answer the questions.
