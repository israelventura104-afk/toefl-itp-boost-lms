TQB-LIST-0131 — Recording Script (Part C)
Topic: Urban Beekeeping and Local Food
Voices: Narrator + Man (Sustainability Speaker)
Target length: ~60–90 seconds

Narrator: Listen to part of a talk at a campus sustainability event.

Man (Speaker): When people picture beekeeping, they often imagine quiet countryside farms.

But a growing number of cities now host rooftop and community-garden hives.

Urban bees can forage on parks, balconies, and street trees—sometimes finding a surprising variety of flowers.

Local honey is one product, yet the bigger story is pollination for neighborhood gardens and small farms at the city edge.

Beekeepers must follow city rules: hive registration, safe distances from sidewalks, and water sources so bees do not crowd fountains.

Neighbors worry about stings, so responsible keepers post signs and choose gentle bee strains.

Pesticides remain a challenge; some cities limit spraying during bloom periods to protect pollinators.

Students here can volunteer with the campus apiary and track bloom calendars for the gardens.

The point is not that every resident should keep bees—it is that pollinators are part of a healthier local food system.

Next month's workshop will cover how to plant a pollinator strip with native flowers.

Narrator: Now get ready to answer the questions.
