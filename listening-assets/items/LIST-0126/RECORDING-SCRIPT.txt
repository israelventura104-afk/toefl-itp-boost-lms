TQB-LIST-0126 — Recording Script (Part C)
Topic: Photosynthesis in Leaf Cells
Voices: Narrator + Woman (Biology Professor)
Target length: ~60–90 seconds

Narrator: Listen to part of a talk in a biology class.

Woman (Professor): Today I want to focus on what happens inside a leaf when sunlight hits it.

Photosynthesis is the process plants use to convert light energy into chemical energy stored as sugar.

Most of the work takes place in chloroplasts—tiny green structures packed with chlorophyll.

Chlorophyll absorbs mainly blue and red wavelengths; green light is mostly reflected, which is why leaves look green to us.

In the first stage, called the light-dependent reactions, water molecules are split and oxygen is released as a by-product.

That stage also produces energy carriers—ATP and NADPH—that power the next stage.

In the Calvin cycle, carbon dioxide from the air is fixed into sugar using those energy carriers.

Notice the plant does not “eat” sunlight the way animals eat food; it transforms light into a usable chemical form.

If a plant gets too little light, sugar production slows, and growth can stall even when water and soil nutrients are available.

Next class we'll look at how temperature and carbon dioxide levels change the rate of the Calvin cycle.
