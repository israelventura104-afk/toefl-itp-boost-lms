Correlative Agreement one skill

With either...or and neither...nor, match the verb to the subject nearest it.

1 Teach2 Demo3 Practice

Today

Learn the nearest-subject rule, see it in action, then test yourself.

Goal today: identify the pair, then let the noun closest to the verb control agreement.

1. Find the pairEither...or, neither...nor, or both...and.
2. Find the nearer nounIt decides singular or plural.

High-yield patterns

  • Either...or → agree with the nearer subject.
    Either the instructor or the students are responsible.
  • Neither...nor → agree with the nearer subject.
    Neither the students nor the instructor is available.
  • Both...and → plural.
    Both the lecture and the workshop are required.
  • As well as is not a correlative pair.
    The instructor, as well as the students, is ready.
Remember

The first subject may be a trap. Look immediately before the verb.

Three model decisions

Either the assistant or the professors ____ the report.

review — professors is nearest and plural.

Neither the tutors nor the coordinator ____ available.

is — coordinator is nearest and singular.

Both the lecture and the workshop ____ required.

are — both...and is plural.

Practice · choose first, then open feedback

1. Neither the manager nor the employees ____ prepared.

are — use the noun nearest the verb.

2. Either the employees or the manager ____ the form.

approves — use the noun nearest the verb.

3. Both the guide and the tourists ____ early.

arrive — use the noun nearest the verb.

4. Either the chair or the members ____ the agenda.

approve — use the noun nearest the verb.

5. Neither the students nor the professor ____ absent.

was — use the noun nearest the verb.

6. Both the assistant and the researcher ____ completed the task.

have — use the noun nearest the verb.

7. Either the laboratories or the library ____ open.

is — use the noun nearest the verb.

8. Neither the library nor the laboratories ____ open.

are — use the noun nearest the verb.

9. Correct: Neither the staff nor the director were notified.

were → was — use the noun nearest the verb.

10. Correct: Both the dean and the advisers has arrived.

has → have — use the noun nearest the verb.

Target

Answer at least 8 correctly without opening the explanations.
